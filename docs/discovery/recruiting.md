# Recruiting interviewees (P0-12)

Goal: **8 completed interviews** with people who match the ICP. Expect roughly 1 call per 5–8
cold messages and more from warm introductions, so plan on 40–60 messages. Book the first
calls before writing more outreach: they will improve the message.

## 1. Who qualifies (screen before booking)

**In: the ICP.** All three must hold:
- Buy-side credit work: a direct lender or private credit fund, a BDC, a high-yield or
  leveraged-loan fund (including CLO managers), a credit hedge fund, or a leveraged-finance
  desk at a bank (underwriting or portfolio management).
- The person's own job includes reading issuers' earnings releases or filings and judging
  EBITDA: underwriting, monitoring or relative value.
- The role is analyst, associate, VP, portfolio manager, or credit / underwriting lead. A PM
  or head of credit counts double for the buying-process questions.

**Out, or count separately:**
- Equity analysts. They are the *fallback persona*: interview them only if the rule fails.
- Rating-agency analysts. Note them separately; they are a useful signal but not the buyer.
- Vendors, data providers, consultants, students.
- Anyone who will not discuss their own workflow.

Ask one screening question before booking: *"In a typical quarter, how do you get from an
issuer's reported Adjusted EBITDA to the number you actually use?"* Someone with no answer
is not the ICP.

Mix to aim for across the 8:
- at least 3 private credit / direct lending / BDC;
- at least 2 HY or leveraged loan;
- at most 2 sell-side or bank leveraged finance.

## 2. Channels (in order of expected yield)

1. **Warm introductions.** Former colleagues, classmates and friends in finance. Ask each
   person for *one* introduction to a credit analyst, not a list.
2. **LinkedIn search.** Titles such as "credit analyst", "private credit", "direct lending",
   "leveraged finance", "high yield analyst", "BDC", "CLO analyst", "underwriting". Prefer
   2nd-degree connections and mention the shared connection.
3. **Alumni networks.** Your university's and former employers' alumni directories and
   groups, filtered by credit roles.
4. **Professional associations and events.** Local CFA Society chapters (fixed-income /
   credit events) and loan-market and private-credit association events and webinars. Speak
   to people in person; do not scrape attendee lists.
5. **Public writing.** People who post or write about credit, covenants or EBITDA add-backs:
   newsletters, LinkedIn posts, X/Twitter. Reply to something specific they wrote.
6. **Referrals from interviewees.** End every call by asking who else should be in this
   study (see the script, §7).

Do not:
- buy contact lists;
- use scraped emails;
- message people at firms where you have a conflict;
- offer payment beyond a token thank-you that the interviewee's employer allows (many firms
  forbid gifts).

Offer what you can give: the anonymized summary of findings, and early access.

## 3. Outreach messages (send them yourself; edit freely)

Fill in the brackets. Keep it short and specific. Do not claim anything that is not true
today: there is no product yet, and no published accuracy number may be shown before P4-13.

**Cold (LinkedIn / email), ~90 words**

> Subject: 30 min on how you adjust EBITDA? (research, not a sales call)
>
> Hi [first name], I'm [your name], building a tool that tracks how US issuers define and
> adjust EBITDA from their filings: each add-back, quarter by quarter, linked to the source.
> I'm talking to credit analysts before building more, to learn how you handle add-backs and
> definition changes today and what is painful about it. Would you have 30 minutes in the
> next two weeks? No pitch. I'll share the anonymized findings with everyone who takes part.
> [Your name] · [one-line credential or link]

**Warm introduction (sent by your contact)**

> [Contact], could you introduce me to someone on your credit team? I'm researching how
> credit analysts rebuild and normalize EBITDA bridges from earnings releases. It's a
> 30-minute conversation, not a sales call. Happy to send a two-line blurb.

**Follow-up (once, after 5–7 days)**

> Hi [first name], following up once in case this got buried. Even 20 minutes on how you
> handle EBITDA add-backs would help a lot. If it's not a fit, no reply needed.

**Booking confirmation**

> Thanks, [first name]. Booked for [date/time, time zone]. I'll ask about your workflow
> around earnings, add-backs and the tools you use. I'd like to take notes; nothing is
> attributed to you or your firm without asking. [Calendar link / dial-in]
