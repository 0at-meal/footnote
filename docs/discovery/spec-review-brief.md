**Thank you for agreeing to look at this.** It should take 30–45 minutes. Short, blunt
comments are the most useful kind.

## What this is

Footnote is building a record of how US-listed issuers define and adjust EBITDA. Each
quarter it captures every line of an issuer's reported EBITDA bridge from its earnings
release, checks the bridge's arithmetic and its GAAP starting point, and links every number
to the exact text on sec.gov.

On top of that record we want to publish a **normalized EBITDA**. It applies one transparent
policy to every issuer, so issuers can be compared like for like. Each add-back the policy
reverses is itemized, and a firm will be able to substitute its own policy.

The attached draft (v0.1) defines that policy:
- **§4:** the add-back categories;
- **§5:** what the default does with each one (accept, reverse or flag);
- **§6:** a mechanical rule for "non-recurring" items that keep recurring;
- **§7:** the calculation;
- **§9:** six worked examples using real bridges from recent releases.

Nothing is computed from it yet. Your critique decides what version 0.2 says before anything
is built on it.

## What we'd like from you

Please read §4–§9 (about 6 pages) and answer the form. The questions that matter most:

1. **Recurring "non-recurring" items (§6, Example 9.1).** We would reverse a category of
   add-backs the issuer has used in at least 3 of the last 4 quarters. In the Chemours
   example that turns reported Adjusted EBITDA of 247 into −125. Is that a rule you would
   trust? What would you change: threshold, window, materiality, exceptions?
2. **Stock-based compensation.** For credit work, should the default accept the add-back,
   reverse it, or only flag it?
3. **The categories (§4).** Is anything missing, merged wrongly, or split needlessly? Would
   you map any of the example lines differently?
4. **Consolidated vs. attributable, EBITDA vs. EBITDAR (Examples 9.3 and 9.6).** Which
   should the default return?
5. **Usefulness.** Would a published default policy be useful to you, or only your own
   firm's policy applied to the same data?

The form has room for the other open questions (§12) and for anything else.

## Practicalities

- **Format:** fill in the form, mark up the PDF, or talk it through for 20 minutes, whichever
  is easiest.
- **Confidentiality:** your comments are recorded under a reviewer ID, not your name or firm.
  Nothing is quoted publicly without your permission.
- **What happens next:** we revise the method to v0.2, answer every comment in writing, and
  send you the revised version and the changes.
- **Status of the numbers:** the bridges in the examples are real public filings. The draft
  category assignments are illustrative, not verified product output, and no accuracy claim
  is made.
